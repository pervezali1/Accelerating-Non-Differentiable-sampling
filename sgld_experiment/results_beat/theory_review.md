# Independent theory review of the parameter-range claims

Three agents were given the claims (A) linear window, (B) axis theorem and (C) accuracy-gap formula
as stated for the FIRST 'scaled' design (slow direction on a coordinate axis, oblique to the L1 axis),
and asked to derive, verify numerically and refute.  Their structured reports are reproduced verbatim.
The corrections they supply (exact stability edge, discrete rate maximal at sigma*, oblique-axis cap
1/cos^2(theta), fast-pair stability limit, v_j Delta_j(0)^2 as the right residual measure, and the
state-dependent axis being democratic early on) are what led to the in-plane design of
`nonreversible_beats_reversible.py`, in which the slow direction is placed exactly in the rotated plane.

## Agent A — linear-algebra route

### A_verdict

TRUE WITH CORRECTIONS. The continuous-time statement is exact; the discrete-time window is right in form but (i) its formula is slightly off, (ii) the window is not flat: the best discrete rate is exactly at sigma* and degrades monotonically above it, (iii) the stability formula uses the wrong eigenvalue pair whenever the axis is oblique, which is the case for BOTH geometries in the scaled design, and (iv) the 'pinned by projection' ending is not what happens.

### A_corrections

Derived (exact, 3x3, axis v_hat = q_1 so q_2,q_3 span the rotated plane; h := eta*a): (I-J)H has eigenvalues l1 and (l2+l3)/2 +- sqrt((l2-l3)^2/4 - sigma^2 l2 l3), so sigma* = (l2-l3)/(2 sqrt(l2 l3)) and Re = (l2+l3)/2 above it -- as claimed. Discrete: |1-h mu|^2 = 1 - h(l2+l3) + h^2 l2 l3 (1+sigma^2) for sigma >= sigma*, hence (a) the EXACT stability edge is sigma_max^2 = (l2+l3)/(h l2 l3) - 1 (claim drops the -1; numerically 10.259 vs 10.308 for l=(100,16,1), h=0.01); (b) the window is non-empty iff h(l2+l3) < 4 exactly (claim: 'roughly h l2 < 4'; verified sign change between h(l2+l3)=3.96 and 4.04); (c) NEW: rho^2 grows linearly in sigma^2 above sigma*, so the discrete rate is maximal exactly at sigma* (rho = |1-h(l2+l3)/2|, measured speed-up 8.84 vs (kappa+1)/2 = 8.5) and decays: sigma = 1.5 sigma* -> 8.4x, 3 sigma* -> 6.2x, 0.9 sigma_max -> 1.6x; NR beats REV in spectral radius only for sigma^2 < (l2-l3)(1-h l3)/(h l2 l3) (numeric 9.633 vs formula 9.634). (d) NEW, and decisive for the experiments: if v_hat is not an eigenvector of H, the slow eigenvalue saturates as sigma -> inf at 1/(v_hat^T H^{-1} v_hat) (verified: 45 deg between q1,q3 -> 1.97 vs formula 1.98; 30 deg from q3 -> 1.327 vs 1.329), i.e. the attainable speed-up is ~1/cos^2(theta), theta = angle(v_hat, q_slow); and the discrete instability is set by the pair of eigenvalues of H restricted to the plane perpendicular to v_hat, sigma_max^2 = (l_a+l_b)/(h l_a l_b) - 1, which for an oblique axis is dominated by the fast directions (45 deg case unstable between sigma = 2.8 and 5.6 while the (l2,l3) formula says 10.3). If q_3 IS the axis, the slow rate is untouched for all sigma and the fast pair goes unstable at sigma ~ 2.8 (l=(100,16,1), h=0.01). (e) 'overshoot, hit the constraint, projection rate -> 1' is refuted in the scaled design: the s=4, eta=3e-5 run is past the linear edge (full 9x9 rho = 1.004) yet projection_rate_nr = 0.0; the instability is contained by the saturating logistic gradient and shows up as a persistent accuracy deficit of -0.018 to -0.027 (t = -3.6 to -9.9) from iteration 160 to 400, not as pinning. Scripts: /tmp/theory_linalg/claimA.py, claimB2.py.

### B_verdict

TRUE WITH CORRECTIONS. The algebra (J_I v perpendicular to v_I, J_I v_I = 0, w*^T(I-J)H x = w*^T H x on the ball) is exact and the ball does fail in the scaled design (speed-up ceiling 1.02-1.04, full-matrix 1.009). But the reason is the ANGLE between w*_I and the slow eigenvector, not 'signal = w*' per se, and the L1 'sqrt(1-1/m) in the plane' is optimistic: at epsilon = 0.2 the axis is not democratic and the in-plane component is 0.60, giving a speed-up ceiling of 1.5, not 3.

### C_verdict

TRUE WITH ONE FATAL PARAMETER ERROR AND TWO CONCEPTUAL CORRECTIONS. The loss formula f(0) delta^2/4 is exact to first order (verified by quadrature), the gap mechanism is real and large (+0.064 to +0.070, t = 6.7-7.8, visible unzoomed) and it lives in the predicted transient window -- but the prescribed s = 4 at eta = 3e-5 is just past the stability edge and LOSES (-0.023, t = -7.9 at iteration 400); s = 2.5-3 (or s = 4 at eta = 1.5e-5) realises the claim. ### parameter_range

Notation: h = eta*a (a = exp(-lambda Sum_j (sqrt(w_j^2+delta^2)-|w_j|)) in [e^{-8 lambda delta}, 1], = 0.98 at the mode here); per block I, H_I = Q diag(l_fast >= l_mid >= l_slow) Q^T at the mode; v_I the block axis (s w_I on the ball, -s soft-sign_eps(w_I) under L1), v_hat its direction, theta = angle(v_hat, q_slow); l_a, l_b the eigenvalues of H_I restricted to the plane perpendicular to v_hat; lambda_min the smallest eigenvalue of the full 9x9 H (124 here, mixing intercept and the two variance-1 slopes). NR beats REV on single-iterate test accuracy iff ALL of: (1) Anisotropy: a coordinate j with feature variance v_j such that l_slow/l_fast ~ v_j/v_fast <= ~1/8 (kappa >= 8) AND v_j Delta_j(0)^2 >= ~0.3 (initial residual worth >= 0.5 logit-sd), where Delta_j(0) ~ beta_j for origin init or ~ init spread (~0.6 per coordinate for uniform init in the L1 ball of radius 4.45) -- this excludes 'aligned' (v_j = 0.02) and isotropic (kappa = 1). Scaled design: v = (1, 16, 16 | 1, 16, 16 | 1, 16, 16), kappa ~ 17, v_j Delta_j(0)^2 = 0.5-0.9 on w0, w3, w6. (2) Axis: cos^2(theta) <= ~0.5 so the continuous ceiling 1/(cos^2/l_slow + sin^2/l_fast)/l_slow ~ 1/cos^2(theta) >= 2. Ball: cos(theta) = |w*_j|/|w*_I|, so it TIES (gap <= 0.002) whenever the slow coordinate carries the dominant coefficient of its block (scaled: cos = 0.98), and could only work if |w*_j| <= 0.5|w*_I| (then Delta_j(0) must come from the init spread) and w* lies inside the unit ball (here |w*| = 1.58, it does not). L1: cos(theta) = ss_j/|ss_I|, ss_i = w*_i/sqrt(w*_i^2+eps^2); with eps = 0.2 and small coefficients 0.13 this is 0.79 (ceiling 1.5, realised 1.38); eps <= half the smallest active |w*_i| pushes toward the democratic 1/sqrt(m), ceiling m = 3. (3) Block strength: s_max(eta) = sqrt((l_a+l_b)/(h l_a l_b) - 1)/|v_I| (for an oblique axis l_a,l_b ~ the two fast eigenvalues: (2356, 3823) -> s_max = 3.73, full-matrix edge 3.94 at eta = 3e-5; 5.65 at 1.5e-5; scales as 1/sqrt(eta a)). The gap is flat for 0.5 s_max <= s <= 0.95 s_max (s = 2.5 and 3 give +0.064/+0.067 peak, +0.031/+0.030 at it 150), ties for s <= ~0.25 s_max (speed-up < 1.1), and LOSES for s > s_max (s = 4 = 1.02-1.08 s_max at eta = 3e-5: -0.02 to -0.03 from it 130 on, t down to -9.9; the linearisation predicts unbounded growth, the real chain is contained by the saturating gradient, projection rate stays 0). (4) Step: h l_max < 2 (0.124 here) is all that is needed for stability of REV; the NR window then exists automatically since h(l_a+l_b) < 4. (5) Evaluation iteration: the gap is positive for 0.15 <= h lambda_min t <= ~1.0 (iterations 50-250 at eta = 3e-5, 80-480 at 1.5e-5), peaks at h lambda_min t ~ 0.3 (it 85 / 170) at +0.06-0.07, is <= +0.001 (tie) for h lambda_min t >= 1.5 (it >= 300 / 520), and can be slightly negative (linear prediction -0.02, observed -0.005 or less) for h lambda_min t < 0.1. Magnitude cap: Bayes-init = 0.77 - 0.50; the small-delta formula times the transient gives 0.04, the full nonlinear chain 0.064-0.070. (6) lambda, delta: enter only via a (rescales h: a in [0.73, 1]) and the anchor curvature lambda delta^2/(w_j^2+delta^2)^{3/2} (0.4 << l_slow = 205); irrelevant as long as lambda delta << 1 and lambda/delta << l_fast; tested only at lambda = 2, delta = 0.02 -- at lambda/delta >~ 3000 with coefficients near 0 the anchor would create new fast directions (not explored). (7) Observable: single-iterate accuracy, paired over R = 40 with shared noise; the ergodic-average observable was not tested. Ties or losses everywhere else: isotropic design; ball geometry with the dominant coefficient on the slow coordinate; 'aligned' (signal-free AND variance-starved slow direction); s past s_max; evaluation past ~1.5/(h lambda_min).

### loophole_or_refutation

Main loophole: claim (A)'s stability formula uses the (lambda_2, lambda_3) pair, but the axis actually produced by either geometry in the scaled design is oblique (cos theta = 0.98 ball, 0.79 L1), so the rotated plane contains the fast directions and the true edge is s_max = sqrt((l_a+l_b)/(eta a l_a l_b) - 1)/|v_I| ~ 3.7-3.9 at eta = 3e-5, not 13. Claim (C)'s recipe (s = 4, eta = 3e-5) therefore sits just past the edge and is REFUTED as stated: the paired gap is -0.023 (t = -7.9) at iteration 400 and never significantly positive; the identical recipe with s = 2.5 or 3, or with eta halved, gives +0.064 to +0.070 (t = 6.7-7.8), so the mechanism itself is confirmed once s is placed inside the corrected window. Second loophole: the 'signal-carrying' requirement in (B)/(C) is design-specific. The ball's failure is a geometric coincidence of the scaled design (the big coefficient sits on the slow coordinate, making w*_I parallel to q_slow), and with uniform initialisation Delta_j(0) is the init spread (~0.6), not beta_j; the linearised model predicts an even larger gap (+0.09) for a design whose slow coordinates are signal-free but oblique to the L1 axis (untested). The correct necessary condition is v_j Delta_j(0)^2 = O(1) together with cos^2(theta) <= 1/2. Third: the 'projection rate -> 1 pinning' description of what happens beyond sigma_max is refuted here (projection rate 0.0 in the unstable run; the instability manifests as a persistent 0.02-0.03 accuracy deficit contained by the logistic saturation). Fourth, a quantitative caveat on (A): the discrete-time speed-up is not (kappa+1)/2 across the window but only at sigma* for an in-plane axis, and for an oblique axis it saturates at ~1/cos^2(theta) with the optimum pushed to the stability edge; for the L1 geometry at eps = 0.2 that ceiling is 1.5 (realised 1.38), far below the nominal (17+1)/2 = 9.

## Agent B — exactly solvable toy-model route

### A_verdict

TRUE WITH CORRECTIONS. The algebra for an axis exactly perpendicular to the slow eigenvector is exact (sigma*, complex pair with Re=(l2+l3)/2, discrete modulus, sigma_max), but (i) the usable window is sigma ~ sigma*, not [sigma*, sigma_max] (the discrete rate is maximal AT sigma* and degrades monotonically above it), (ii) the 'non-empty iff eta a lambda_2 < 4' condition is vacuous (always true when REV is stable), and (iii) the theorem says nothing about the state-dependent axes actually used, whose relevant limits are a fast-pair stability bound sigma^2 < 2/(eta a lambda_fast) - 1 and a trace-conservation bound rate_slow <= 1/(vhat^T H^-1 vhat) ~ lambda_3/c^2 (c = |vhat . q_3|).

### A_corrections

Verified numerically (3-d diag(16,16,1), eta*l1 in {0.8,1.6,1.92}): sigma* = (l2-l3)/(2 sqrt(l2 l3)) = 1.875; eigenvalues real below, complex with Re = 8.5 = (l2+l3)/2 above; closed form |1 - eta mu|^2 = 1 - eta(l2+l3) + eta^2 l2 l3 (1+sigma^2) (1.71 numeric = 1.71 formula). Exact stability: sigma^2 < (l2+l3)/(eta a l2 l3) - 1 (the claim omits the -1); window non-empty iff eta a (l2+l3) < 4, which holds automatically whenever eta a l1 < 2, so the condition is never binding. CORRECTION 1 (window shape): discrete rate r(sigma) = -1/2 log(1 - eta a (l2+l3) + (eta a)^2 l2 l3 (1+sigma^2)) is maximal exactly at sigma* and decreases for sigma > sigma*; at 0.9 sigma_max the toy mean residual oscillates (+0.24, +0.71, -0.16, ...) with net rate 0.89x REV, i.e. worse than reversible. Usable window is sigma in [~0.5 sigma*, ~2 sigma*], best at sigma*. CORRECTION 2 (speed-up value): (kappa+1)/2 is the continuous-time ratio; the discrete ratio at sigma* is log(1-eta a(l2+l3)/2)/log(1-eta a l3) (11.1x at eta l1 = 0.8, kappa=16, vs 8.5), and once the pair is accelerated the overall slowest mode is the un-rotated l1 direction with rate -log|1-eta a l1|, which collapses near the REV stability limit (eta l1 = 1.9: speed-up 0.89). CORRECTION 3 (generic axis, the case that matters): tr[(I-J)H] = tr H for every J (J H has zero diagonal), so accelerating q3 is paid by the other modes; the sigma->inf slow real eigenvalue is exactly 1/(vhat^T H^-1 vhat) <= l3/c^2 with c = |vhat.q3| (verified: 2.667 for c=1/sqrt3, 1.509 for c=0.8 at kappa=16; 46/50.5 for c=0, kappa=100). Best achievable discrete slow speed-up (scan over sigma, all three modes stable): c=0: 8.7 (eta l1=0.1), 12.1 (eta l1=1.0); c=0.577 (democratic): 2.62 (eta l1=0.1, sigma=6.3), 2.50 (0.3), 2.29 (0.6), 1.97 (1.0), 1.49 (1.5); c=0.8 (proposed config's soft-sign axis): 1.49, 1.46, 1.40, 1.31, 1.18. So for any axis not perpendicular to q3, kappa is almost irrelevant and eta a lambda_fast must be SMALL (<~0.3), the opposite of claim (C)'s 'eta near the fast-direction stability limit'. CORRECTION 4 (second stability limit): a generic axis also rotates the two fast directions; their pair is stable only for sigma^2 < (l1+l2)/(eta a l1 l2) - 1 ~ 2/(eta a l_fast) - 1, which binds long before the slow-pair sigma_max: sigma_max,fast = 2.4 at eta l_f = 0.3 (toy: soft-sign axis sigma=3.5, ball s=4, L1 s=4 and s=8 all diverge), 4.1 at eta a l_f = 0.11 (real proposed config: s=4 gives sigma=4.9 -> NR slowest mode 0.0023/it vs REV 0.007/it, and the whole-9-d linearisation is unstable). ### loophole_or_refutation

Refuted: (A)'s window sigma* <= sigma <= sigma_max and its (kappa+1)/2 speed-up do not apply to either geometry in the scaled design because the rotation axis has a large component along the slow direction; the binding constraint is the fast-pair discrete stability s phi_slow < sqrt(2/(eta a lambda_fast) - 1) (3x narrower than sigma_max) and the attainable speed-up is <= 1/phi_hat_par^2 ~ 1.65 at the mode. Empirically s = 4 and s = 6, both well inside the 2x2 window, make NR lose (-0.023) or collapse (-0.16, pinned). (C)'s prescribed configuration (s = 4, 'eta near the fast limit') is therefore refuted as stated: it yields an insignificant peak (+0.011, t 0.9) and a significant LOSS at the evaluation iteration; raising eta shrinks the window, halves the peak and adds a stationary deficit. Attacks (ii) projection and (iii) a(w) fail: the mode is interior with margin 2.1, projection rates are 0.000 in all winning and marginal runs, and a in [0.73, 1] is a common time change of both samplers (0.98 at the mode). Attack (v) init is not a legitimate refutation: both samplers share the uniform-on-K start, which is the standard fair init, and the claim is explicitly transient; it is legitimate only as a caveat that the gap size is an init property (sum v_j Delta_j(0)^2 = 3.9 here) and that NR ties or slightly loses once stationary. Attack (vi) ball rescue fails quantitatively: putting the slow direction perpendicular to w*_I makes it rotatable but leaves it with initial residual 1/(d+2) = 0.09 and predictive weight v_s beta_s^2, so the gap is O(0.001). The loophole that SAVES (C): attack (i), the state dependence of the axis, works in NR's favour rather than against it. With uniform init every |w_i(0)| ~ 0.57 exceeds eps, the soft-sign axis is democratic (cap 3, linearised speed-up 2.6x at such a point with s = 3) while the fast coordinates converge, and only later de-saturates to the mode value (cap 1.65); this is why the measured peak (0.067 +- 0.010 at iteration 85, s = 3, eta = 3e-5) exceeds the mode-linearised estimate (~0.035) and clears the 0.03 threshold. Surviving claims: the ball part of (B) in full; the mechanism of (C) and its existence claim (gap > 0.03, visible unzoomed) in the corrected window s ~ 3 (0.75-0.9 of the fast-pair limit), eta a lambda_fast ~ 0.1, iterations 50-140, with honest upper bound ~0.07-0.08 for this design and init (trivial cap 0.30); the 2x2 algebra of (A) as a conditional statement whose condition (axis = fast eigenvector) is not met here.
